I am an archaeologist with more than 15 years of experience working at the crossroads of fieldwork, laboratory analysis, and digital research. My career began with excavations and survey projects in Germany, Cameroon, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo, where I quickly became fascinated by how people’s everyday technologies reflect wider histories of settlement and exchange. Over the years, I have specialized in studying pottery traditions in Central Africa, using a combination of technological analysis (*chaîne opératoire* and macro-/meso-trace studies) and archaeometric methods such as mineralogy and portable X-ray fluorescence (pXRF). These approaches allow me to retrace how communities learned, adapted, and transmitted knowledge across generations.

Alongside field and lab work, I have always been deeply invested in the digital side of archaeology. At the University of Tübingen, I helped design and build a virtual research environment (*Spacialist*) for managing complex archaeological data, and since then I have continued to integrate GIS, database programming, and statistical tools (R, Python, SQL) into my research. At Ghent University, I expanded this expertise through projects that connected ceramic analysis with broader questions about networks of knowledge and cultural change in the Congo Basin. Most recently, I have also applied my geophysical surveying skills in a professional setting with ADEDE Search & Recovery, bridging academic research and practical archaeology. Across all of these roles, I thrive on combining hands-on investigation with innovative methods to tell richer stories about the past.
